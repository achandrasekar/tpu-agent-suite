## Grounding

Anchor each claim to the section of the page it came from, by that section's
own heading, so a later reader can re-find it without re-reading the page.

Three kinds of gap, three different answers, and they must not be blurred:

- **The page is silent.** Leave the template's `not stated`. It is the correct
  answer, and the one thing a later reader cannot recover once a guess sits in
  its place.
- **You know something useful the page does not say.** Put it in a
  `> [!inferred]` block: hedged and visible, neither deleted nor promoted to a
  cited fact.
- **You are unsure whether the page says it.** Then it does not. Re-read before
  writing, rather than resolving it in the entry's favour.

**Any number carries the configuration it was measured at, or it does not
appear.** A throughput or speedup figure was measured at some hardware, shape,
precision and baseline. Give those with it; where the page omits them, report
the figure as unattributed rather than as a property of the thing.

Quote identifiers, flags and figures in the page's own form. Do not shorten,
expand, combine, convert or round them -- the reader can do arithmetic, and
they cannot undo yours.

## Before you answer

does this page positively say it, in this order, under these conditions? Not
"is it plausible" -- plausible is the failure mode, not the test.
